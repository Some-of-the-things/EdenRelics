Before you use this

Three things worth knowing up front. None of them are buried in the terms, because you would find them out eventually anyway — and finding them out the hard way is how people end up distrusting a tool that was being straight with them all along.

Vinted doesn’t officially permit automation

We keep posting human-paced to minimise the risk — one listing at a time, filled at the speed a person types, with a gap in between. But your account is yours and the risk is real. Vinted’s enforcement is automated and hard to appeal, and it weighs patterns rather than single actions.

That is also why this extension never presses publish for you. It fills the form. You look at it and publish it yourself. If that ever changes, we have broken our own rule.

Sales on Vinted and Depop only sync while your browser is open

Neither platform gives us a way to check from our server, so the extension looks while Chrome is running and not otherwise. If a piece sells on Vinted at 3am with your laptop shut, we will not know until you next open it — and until then, it can still be showing as available somewhere else.

Etsy and eBay do have proper APIs. Those we watch continuously from our own server, whether your machine is on or not, and a sale there triggers delisting elsewhere.

We never hold your marketplace passwords

The extension works inside the sessions you are already signed in to. It does not ask for, send or store a Vinted or Depop password, and there is nowhere in Eden Relics that one could be kept. The only credential it holds is your Eden Relics session, and you can disconnect it from the extension’s menu at any time.

How a listing actually goes out

  1. You press Send to extension on a piece in Eden Relics.
  2. The extension opens that platform’s new-listing page.
  3. It fills the fields, one at a time, at a human pace.
  4. It tells you it has finished and stops.
  5. You check the form and press publish.

If any of that fails — a field we can’t find, a form that has been redesigned, a page that won’t load — you get told exactly what didn’t happen, and the listing text to paste in yourself. You should never be left thinking something went live when it didn’t.

Some platforms are not ready yet. Where we haven’t finished recording what a platform’s form actually asks for, the extension refuses to touch it and hands you the text instead. That is deliberate: a tool that quietly fills the wrong fields is worse than one that admits it can’t.
